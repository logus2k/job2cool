\section{Reflection on the Three-Stage Arc}
\label{sec:arc}

This reflection is part of the grade: which stage contributed most, and were earlier stages
washed out by later ones?

\paragraph{The A1/A2 work is directly visible, not diluted.}
\verb|ma2-360m-dpo-b01| is literally the Job-Offer drafter inside the running system, not a
discarded checkpoint. A1 made a 360M model fluent in job-posting language (in-domain
perplexity 16.37 down to 11.38); A2 taught it to follow recruiter instructions and aligned
its output to a quality rubric (SFT validation perplexity 4.54; DPO-b01 the $\beta$=0.10
deliverable, 8 wins to 0 against SFT); and A3 placed that specialist inside a system that
can finally use it on a real task.

\paragraph{Did RAG make domain adaptation redundant?}
No: they operate on different axes. A1/A2 improved the writing quality and structure of one
output in isolation, while RAG supplies what no amount of fine-tuning a 360M model can give,
namely knowledge of \emph{this company's} actual material and verifiable provenance (a claim
traced to a PDF page and bbox). A fine-tuned model can write a fluent onboarding plan; it
cannot write this company's onboarding plan, nor prove where a requirement came from. They
are complementary, not substitutes.

\paragraph{Was the A2 alignment washed out by gemma's composition?}
This is the sharpest question, and the system is built to answer it: when both are selected,
the raw A2 draft and the gemma-composed Job Offer are produced side by side (the
Section~\ref{sec:eval} (b)-versus-(c) view). The honest expectation, given a 4B composer
refining a 360M draft, is \emph{some} masking, with the final prose leaning toward
\verb|gemma-4|. But the A2 model's contribution is structural: it reliably produces the four
required sections in the required Markdown, aligned to recruiter preferences, and that
structure persists as the seed the composer refines. The value of A2 in this architecture is
a fast (about 0.7\,s), structurally-correct, aligned seed that anchors the composition, not
the source of the final wording. Reporting that masking openly, rather than claiming the
360M model dominates the output, is the honest reading.

\paragraph{A1's marginal contribution under a 4B model with RAG.}
The most genuinely open question is whether A1's raw posting-fluency contribution stays
clearly non-redundant once \verb|gemma-4| and RAG are in the loop, since retrieved company
postings could in principle teach the composer the style A1 baked into the 360M model. A1's
contribution is most clearly non-redundant for the A2 instruction-following and alignment it
enabled, and least clearly so for raw fluency in this configuration, a tension worth stating
rather than papering over, and one a future A1/no-A1 toggle could quantify just as the
MA2/Gemma toggle quantifies the A2 question.

\paragraph{Which stage contributed most?}
For the final system's \emph{usefulness}, A3 contributed most: it is the orchestration,
retrieval, grounding, and platform that turned an aligned drafter into something a recruiter
can operate. For the specific quality of the Job Offer deliverable, A1 and A2 contributed
most. These are not in competition. An aligned model with no system is a good drafter no one
can operate; a system with no aligned model has no specialist at its core. The most
defensible single sentence is that the final stage did not make the earlier stages
redundant: it gave them somewhere to be useful.
